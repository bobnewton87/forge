# Raising your own testosterone: what the evidence actually supports

Prepared for Bob (39, former 2:30 marathoner, ~7 months lifting, ~164 lb at ~23% DXA body fat, Adderall).
Written 2026-10-19 and substantially extended 2026-10-20 (interaction model, the Finkelstein fat term, the planner,
oral and nasal testosterone, and several corrections — see §7–§10c) by the Forge research builder. Every study below was opened and checked; conversions use
ng/dL = nmol/L × 28.84. **Not medical advice.** Everything in the prescription section needs a physician,
a diagnosis and lab monitoring — none of it is appropriate to self-direct, and several items are
investigational. Items marked UNVERIFIED could not be confirmed from a primary source and are not modeled.

---

## 1. Executive summary — what actually moves testosterone, ranked

**The honest headline: you are the wrong patient for almost all of this.** Nearly every therapy that raises
testosterone was tested in men who were hypogonadal, obese, or both. In men already in the normal range, the
pharmacological options either have no data or have data showing nothing happens. And the one hard lesson
from the trials that *did* measure muscle is that a bigger testosterone number does not reliably become more
muscle: two year-long randomized trials of aromatase inhibitors raised testosterone 60%+ and found **zero**
change in body composition and strength, while spine bone density fell.

Ranked by how much they move serum total T, with who they move it in:

| # | Lever | Typical change | In whom | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Stop under-eating** | Reverses losses of 40–75% | Lean men in large deficits — *this is your lever* | Moderate |
| 2 | **hCG** | ~+150% | Men with low T | Moderate (1 RCT) |
| 3 | **Letrozole / anastrozole** | +31% to +150% (and +58% in eugonadal men) | Low-T men, obese men, and healthy young men | Strong for T, **measured and null for muscle**, and a modeled fat cost |
| 4 | **Clomiphene** | **+274 ng/dL (95% CI 192–356)** | Low T with a working pituitary | Strong for T |
| 5 | **Fat loss, if obese** | +83 ng/dL (diet), +252 (surgery) | BMI ≥30 only | Strong |
| 6 | **Enclomiphene** | +216 ng/dL (95% CI 188–245) | Low T, overweight | Moderate; not an approved drug |
| 7 | **Tamoxifen** | ~+61% (490→790 ng/dL) | Eugonadal infertile men | Moderate for T; no muscle data |
| 8 | **Cut endurance volume** | Chronic deficit of 25–40% | Years of high-volume endurance — *possibly you* | Weak (cross-sectional) |
| 9 | **Fix short sleep** | ~+10–15% | Men sleeping ~5 h | Moderate |
| 10 | **Treat high prolactin** | Normalizes T in ~61% | Men with hyperprolactinemia only | Moderate |
| 11 | **Cut daily alcohol** | ~+7% | Daily drinkers | Moderate |

| 13 | **Ashwagandha** | ~+10–15% (SMD 0.33, CI 0.13–0.54) | Novices, overweight middle-aged men | Weak |
| 14 | **Tongkat ali** | ~+15% | Only men under ~300 ng/dL | Weak |
| — | **Everything else sold for this** | 0% | Everyone | See §4 |
| — | **Kisspeptin analogs** | **Negative** with continuous dosing | Healthy men | Refuted for this purpose |

**Three things worth your attention, in order:** (1) eat enough, (2) sleep, (3) get labs before assuming
anything. The rest is noise at your likely baseline.

## 2. Prescription and investigational therapies

All of these work by pushing the hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal (HPG) axis rather than replacing testosterone.
That matters for one concrete reason: **testosterone therapy shuts down sperm production.** It was tested as a
male contraceptive and worked — in WHO trials, 200 mg/week produced azoospermia in two-thirds of men and sperm
counts under 3 million/mL in 96–98% (WHO Task Force, *Int J Androl* 1995, PMID 7558380; *Fertil Steril* 1996,
PMID 8654646). The Endocrine Society guideline says plainly: do not start testosterone in a man planning
fertility in the near term (Bhasin 2018, PMID 29562364). The SERMs and hCG below raise LH and FSH instead,
so sperm production is preserved or improved.

### Clomiphene citrate — the best-evidenced option
- **Hohl 2025 meta-analysis** (10 RCTs, 819 men, functional/secondary hypogonadism, baseline 67–303 ng/dL;
  PMID 41066380): total T **+273.8 ng/dL (95% CI 191.9–355.7)**, LH +4.66 IU/L, FSH +4.59, estradiol +34 pg/mL.
  GRADE moderate. Benefit shrank with higher age (p=0.043) and higher BMI (p=0.0008). Equal to testosterone gel
  (difference 5.41 ng/dL, CI −43.4 to +54.3). Free T SMD 1.57 (0.44–2.70), DHT +7.58 ng/dL (3.42–11.73). SHBG did
  NOT move significantly (+3.63 nmol/L, CI −0.54 to 7.81, p=0.09). The meta-analysis did not compare SERMs with
  injections; an earlier draft of this document said it did, which was wrong. Against hCG (Habous 2018, the only
  RCT): SERM +158, SERM+hCG +153, hCG +134 ng/dL, and the combination was no better than the SERM alone (p=0.57).
- **Fertility:** SERM vs gel kept sperm concentration **+70.4 million/mL** higher (CI 41.6–99.2) and cut the risk
  of falling under 15 million/mL by 90% (RR 0.10, CI 0.04–0.23).
- **Body composition — one trial only.** Soares 2018 (RCT, 78 men with obesity, baseline ~220 ng/dL, 12 weeks;
  PMID 29777228) found improved lean, fat-free and muscle mass (all p≤0.004) — **by bioimpedance, not DXA**,
  in obese hypogonadal men. HDL fell. No other clomiphene study measured muscle.
- **IT WORKS AT A NORMAL BASELINE TOO — the 2026-10-20 correction.** Bob: *"the clomiphene thing — I bet that
  works for anyone, bet there are hypertrophic effects to anyone, and we should model that."* On the hormone he
  is right, and the previous model was wrong. Two studies in men who were explicitly **healthy and eugonadal**:

  **Caveat on the 1987 "+304%".** The abstract's headline figure comes from a Pearlman mass-action calculation
  that ignores albumin and puts baseline bioavailable T at 12.9% of total — far below the usual 35–50%. The same
  paper also reports the **Södergard** calculation (the standard one), and that is the number this document uses:
  the bioavailable **fraction did not change**. Do not quote the 304%.
- **So does free testosterone actually rise?** Yes, and this is the direct answer to the obvious objection that a
  SERM raises total T, estradiol *and* SHBG together. Four strands agree: (1) Bremner 1987's unchanged
  bioavailable fraction above; (2) SHBG did not move at all over 8 weeks in Tenover 1991, and the pooled change
  across 10 RCTs was not significant (+3.63 nmol/L, p=0.09, Hohl 2025) — the rise Marshall 1972
  (doi:10.1677/joe.0.0530261) first described is early and modest; (3) Soares 2018 measured free T rising
  (p<0.001) *even though* SHBG rose in the same arm; (4) Guay 1995 (double-blind placebo-controlled crossover,
  PMID 8530597) and Guay 2003 (n=178, doi:10.1038/sj.ijir.3900981) both found free T up significantly.
  **Validation:** feeding Bremner's measured numbers (490 → 980 ng/dL, SHBG 24.5 → 31.3) through Forge's own
  Vermeulen routine gives free T 120.4 → 242.5 pg/mL — a ratio of **2.01** against a total-T ratio of **2.00**.
  The engine reproduces the measurement without being told to. The objection does not bite.
- **A raised LH does not rule you out.** Keihani 2020 (Urology 142:119–24, doi:10.1016/j.urology.2020.04.074;
  332 men, mean age 36.2): total T **+329.2 ng/dL (95% CI 307.4–351.0)**, 73% gained ≥200 ng/dL, and **neither
  baseline LH nor baseline FSH predicted the response**; only age did. Their conclusion: adequate response "can
  be expected in most patients with **normal or slightly elevated** baseline gonadotropin levels". Patel 2015
  (n=47, doi:10.1038/ijir.2015.21) and Roque 2026 (n=60, doi:10.5489/cuaj.9493) found the same. Against that,
  Mazzola 2014 (n=76, doi:10.1111/jsm.12592) found LH ≤6 IU/mL predicted response (HR 3.5) and Kim 2026 (n=292,
  median age 60, 41% post-prostatectomy, doi:10.1093/jsxmed/qdag159) found **OR 0.82 per mIU/mL of baseline LH**
  (CI 0.71–0.95, p=.008) — a gradient, not a cliff. Both Keihani and Kim found **baseline total T was not
  predictive** either.
- **Age, not baseline level, is what shrinks the response.** Tenover 1991 and Bremner 1987 both found young >
  elderly (2.68× vs 1.98×; +100% vs +34%). Hohl 2025: p=0.043 for age, p=0.0008 for BMI. Guay 2003: p<0.05 for
  age. Keihani 2020: age was the only significant univariable predictor.
- **Dose.** There is no within-trial dose ladder for clomiphene in men. 25 mg/day took 36 men (mean age 39) from
  247.6 ± 39.8 to **610.0 ± 178.6 ng/dL** in 4–6 weeks (Shabsigh 2005, doi:10.1111/j.1743-6109.2005.00075.x),
  which is indistinguishable from the 50 mg/day trials — so the curve looks close to flat over 25–50 mg/day. The
  1,390 ng/dL figure comes from **100 mg/day**, two to four times the usual clinical dose, in **five** men. Lim
  1976 (doi:10.1210/jcem-43-6-1370) used the same 100 mg/day in 5 uremic men: 223 → 879 ng/dL. Enclomiphene does
  have a ladder (Wiehle 2013, day-42 morning mean): 6.25 mg → 420 ± 212, 12.5 mg → 404 ± 165, 25 mg → **604 ±
  160** (Cmax **764 ± 164**), gel → 500 ± 278.
- **Risks:** mood changes, visual disturbance (stop and call the prescriber), breast tenderness; ~8% report a
  side effect in long-term series. Estradiol rises. Libido was *lower* than on testosterone therapy
  (MD −0.54, CI −0.87 to −0.21; Constantinou 2026, PMID 42435198). Long-term data are retrospective:
  in 400 men followed ~2 years, 88% became eugonadal with no serious events (Krzastek 2019, PMID 31216250).

### Enclomiphene — same idea, purified, not approved
Pooled +216 ng/dL (CI 188–245) in obesity-related androgen deficiency (Tienforti 2023, PMID 36604313). Two
phase-3 RCTs in overweight men with T ≤300 and LH <9.4 showed levels matching testosterone gel while LH/FSH
rose and sperm counts held (Kim 2016, PMID 26496621). **FDA history:** the advisory committee was cancelled in
Oct 2015 and a Complete Response Letter followed on 30 Nov 2015; it has never been approved. It currently sits
in **Category 1** of the FDA's 503A nominated bulk substances list (verified May 2026), which is how
compounding pharmacies supply it. **No enclomiphene trial has ever measured body composition**, and **no
enclomiphene trial has ever enrolled a eugonadal man** — Wiehle 2013 enrolled 48 and had to drop 4 whose
baseline came back above 350 ng/dL.

Dose ladder, day-42 morning total T (Wiehle 2013, doi:10.1111/bju.12363): 6.25 mg → 420 ± 212, 12.5 mg →
404 ± 165, 25 mg → 604 ± 160 ng/dL, gel → 500 ± 278 (across-group p = 0.07); 24-h means 391 / 426 / 586 vs 546
(p = 0.003); 24-h Cmax 516 / 554 / **764 ± 164** vs 908. Measured as a *rise over each arm's own baseline*, the
two lower arms are indistinguishable at ≈43 % of the 25 mg arm, which is how Forge now models the dose-response
(§12.4). 764 remains the ceiling and 604 the muscle-credit bound; §12.5 says why both survive re-derivation.

**The whole enclomiphene question was re-examined on 2026-10-20 — see [§12](#12-the-isomer-question-is-enclomiphene-better-tolerated-with-less-estrogen-effect-2026-10-20).**
That section replaces the sentence that used to sit here claiming the eugonadal arm was "clomiphene's measured
eugonadal response scaled by 216/274". That description was both circular (Hohl's 274 pools clomiphene *and*
enclomiphene) and a misreading of the arithmetic (the curve is anchored on enclomiphene's own measured +216).
Assumption **A15** is restated accordingly.

### hCG — the only one with a placebo-controlled lean-mass result
**Liu 2002** (double-blind RCT, 40 men >60 with low T, 3 months; PMID 12107212): total and free T up ~150%,
**lean body mass +2 kg (p<0.001), fat mass −1 kg** — and **no improvement in shoulder or knee strength, gait,
balance or physical activity.** Testis volume fell ~5 mL; three men had nipple tenderness. Alongside
testosterone therapy it preserves sperm (Hsieh 2013, PMID 23260550: no man became azoospermic). Since 2020 hCG
is regulated as a biologic, which removed it from ordinary compounding.
**At a normal baseline (2026-10-20 correction):** the old model zeroed hCG above 500 ng/dL. That was the same
unexamined inference as clomiphene's, and it is contradicted by the fact that **hCG stimulation is the standard
clinical test of Leydig-cell reserve in normal men** — Cailleux-Bounacer 2008 (Eur J Endocrinol 159:171–8,
doi:10.1530/EJE-07-0876) showed dose-dependent testosterone and estradiol rises in normal men, peaking 72 h
after the injection, and Panidis 2002 (PMID 11915586) gave 5,000 IU to 30 fertile eugonadal men aged 23–38 and
tracked the rise for a week. But those are **single injections**; nobody has given chronic hCG to a eugonadal
man and measured anything, so Forge's curve above 430 ng/dL is a deliberately conservative **assumption**.

### Aromatase inhibitors — the cautionary tale
They work on the number: anastrozole took men 62–74 from 343±61 to 572±139 ng/dL (+67%; Leder 2004,
PMID 15001605), and weekly letrozole took obese men from 248 to 620 ng/dL (+150%; Loves 2013, PMID 23949882).
Then:
- **Burnett-Bowie 2009** (RCT, 12 months, PMID 18616708): body composition (CT and DXA) and strength **did not
  change.**
- **Burnett-Bowie 2009b** (RCT, 12 months, PMID 19820017): **spine BMD fell** on anastrozole (1.121→1.102 g/cm²)
  while rising on placebo, p=0.0014. "Aromatase inhibition does not improve skeletal health in aging men."
- **Loves 2013:** "significant effects on the predefined endpoints were not observed" despite +150% T.
- **Finkelstein 2013** (NEJM, 400 men, PMID 24024838): androgen deficiency drives loss of lean mass and strength,
  but **estrogen deficiency is what drives fat gain** — so suppressing estradiol works against getting lean.

For an ex-endurance athlete with possible low energy availability, the bone finding is the one that should stop
the conversation.

### Tamoxifen
Krause 1992 (randomized, placebo-controlled, 76 **eugonadal** infertile men; PMID 1544695): T 490 → 790 ng/dL
(+61%) vs 530 → 560 on placebo. This is the only randomized T rise in men who were *not* hypogonadal. A Cochrane
review of 10 trials in 738 men found hormones improved but pregnancy rates did not (OR 1.26, CI 0.99–1.56).
Body composition was never measured. Risks: venous thromboembolism, rare ocular effects.

### Cabergoline — only if prolactin is high
Colao 2004 (51 men with prolactinoma, 24 months; PMID 15070934): prolactin normalized in 76–80%, tumour shrank
~73%, **testosterone normalized in ~61% by 6 months** — 20 of 51 still needed replacement. Irrelevant at a
normal prolactin, which is exactly why prolactin belongs on the lab panel.

### Short-acting nasal testosterone — exogenous, but HPG-sparing
Ramasamy 2020 (open-label single-arm, 33–44 evaluable; PMID 32294396): 90.9% reached T >300 ng/dL while LH
stayed normal in 72.7% and FSH in 81.8%, and sperm counts above 5 M were maintained in 93.9% at 6 months. No
control group, heavy attrition, nothing past 6 months, no body composition.

### Investigational and preclinical
- **Kisspeptin and analogs — refuted for this purpose.** A single infusion raises T 15–45% for hours
  (Dhillo 2005, PMID 16174713: 626→718 ng/dL; George 2011, PMID 21632807: 479→692). But **continuous** dosing
  of the long-acting analogs drove testosterone *below castrate levels* within 7–8 days (Scott 2013,
  PMID 22803642; MacLean 2014, PMID 24762108) — they were being developed as prostate-cancer drugs. MVT-602 has
  published human data only in women; **no male serum-T data exist.** NK3 agonists: none found; the only human
  data are for an NK3 *antagonist*, which cut T by 68% (Anderson 2020, PMID 32946574).
- **Pulsatile GnRH (gonadorelin pump).** Works in congenital hypogonadotropic hypogonadism, but produces
  *lower* T than gonadotropins: 239 vs 467 ng/dL (Mao 2017, PMID 28051040), with faster spermatogenesis
  (Wei 2020 meta-analysis, PMID 32777865). Continuous (non-pulsed) delivery suppresses the axis. Never tested
  in men with a normal pituitary. US availability unclear; gonadorelin does **not** appear in the FDA 503A
  nominated-bulks list as of May 2026, contradicting common secondary claims — its compounding basis is
  UNVERIFIED.
- **GLP-1 drugs.** They do raise T in obesity-related hypogonadism — about +72 to +150 ng/dL across reviews
  (La Vignera 2026, PMID 42653816), with liraglutide +~75 ng/dL vs TRT +~170 in a randomized comparison
  (Jensterle 2019, PMID 30707677) — but meta-regression shows **the rise tracks weight lost, not the drug**
  (Salvio 2025, PMID 40105090). Modeled in Forge as fat loss, not as a separate hormonal effect.
- **Metformin:** no change in T in the one trial found (Krysiak 2021, PMID 32813271); no systematic review
  located. **SGLT2 inhibitors:** no change (Kosinski 2024, PMID 38877312). **Opioid antagonists:** acute LH/T
  rises in old uncontrolled studies, cortisol rose too; nothing durable. Buprenorphine users ran 510 vs
  280 ng/dL for methadone, but that is cross-sectional (Bliesener 2005, PMID 15483091).
- **Leydig stem-cell and gene therapy — preclinical.** Prime-edited stem Leydig cells restored testosterone,
  puberty and fertility in mutant mice (Xia 2023, PMID 37697644); human adipose pericytes failed to become
  Leydig cells in rats (Curley 2019, PMID 31636275). No human trial verified. Nothing to buy; anything sold on
  this basis today is a scam.
- **Post-cycle recovery after anabolic steroids:** of 179 users reviewed, hypogonadism was completely reversed
  in 4 of the 38 with known outcomes (Vilar Neto 2021, PMID 33887077). Worth knowing before, not after.

## 3. Lifestyle — where your actual leverage is

| Lever | Effect on total T | Population | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Energy availability | −40% to −75% when badly under-eating; recovers in days–weeks | Lean, hard-training men | Moderate |
| Endurance volume | −25% to −45% cross-sectionally | Years of high-volume training | Weak–moderate |
| Sleep | −10–15% at 5 h/night; SMD −0.64 for total deprivation | Everyone | Moderate |
| Alcohol | −6.8% at ~3 drinks/day for 3 weeks | Daily drinkers | Moderate |
| Fat loss | +83 ng/dL (diet), +252 (surgery) | BMI ≥30 | Strong |
| Low-fat diets | SMD −0.38 (CI −0.75 to −0.01) going lower-fat | Mostly healthy men | Weak–moderate |
| Resistance training | **No change** in resting T (SMD 0.00, CI −0.20 to 0.20) | Men 19–75 | Strong (null) |
| Stress management, CPAP, time-restricted eating | Null or unproven | — | Weak |

- **Under-eating.** Friedl 2000 (PMID 10797147): Ranger course, ~1,000–1,200 kcal/day deficits plus load and
  sleep loss, testosterone ~130 ng/dL, "approaching castrate", LH suppressed — and **refeeding brought prompt
  recovery** even while the other stressors continued. Rossow 2013 (PMID 23412685): a natural bodybuilder
  prepping to 4.5% body fat went 922 → 227 ng/dL and back to 991 after recovery. Mild short restriction does
  much less (Koehler 2016, PMID 26852783: 4 days at 15 kcal/kg FFM, no T change). Separately, deficits around
  500 kcal/day stop lean-mass gain from lifting (Murphy & Koehler 2021, PMID 34623696).
- **Endurance volume — the "exercise-hypogonadal male condition."** Hackney 1988 (PMID 3343919): 11
  endurance-trained men at 499 vs 725 ng/dL in sedentary controls (−31%), free T −27%. Hooper 2017
  (PMID 28470410): 265 vs 467 ng/dL (−43%), with energy availability 27 vs 45 kcal/kg FFM — under-eating is
  tangled up in it. Hackney 2020 (PMID 32082255) defines it as a persistent 25–50% reduction taking years to
  appear, plateauing ~30–35% lower in a 196-runner cohort. It is at least partly reversible when volume drops
  (Hackney & Hooper 2019, PMID 31847532).
- **Sleep.** Leproult & Van Cauter 2011 (PMID 21632481): one week of 5 h nights cut daytime T 10–13% in
  10 young men (vs 10 h in bed — a strong comparator). Su 2021 meta-analysis (PMID 34801825): partial
  restriction SMD −0.22 (CI −0.50 to 0.06, not significant); total deprivation SMD −0.64 (−0.87 to −0.42).
  No controlled sleep-*extension* trial with T as an outcome exists.
- **Resistance training.** Acute post-workout spikes are real (SMD 0.74, gone in 30 min) and **irrelevant**:
  training one arm under high hormones and one under low produced the same growth (West 2010, PMID 19910330),
  and no hormone response correlated with gains in 49 trained men (Morton 2016, PMID 27174923). Chronic
  resting T does not change with training (Potter 2021, PMID 35134000).
- **Micronutrients.** Zinc restriction crashed 4 young men from 1,151 to 306 ng/dL, and repleting marginally
  deficient older men moved them 239 → 461 (Prasad 1996, PMID 8875519) — tiny, uncontrolled. In replete men,
  nothing (Koehler 2009; Wilborn 2004). Vitamin D is null in meta-analysis (Hosseini Marnani 2019: MD 0.20
  nmol/L, CI −0.20 to 0.60; Paez-Allendes 2026: 0.47, CI −0.50 to 1.44, GRADE low), including in men with
  low T (Lerchbaum 2019).

Product audits put this in perspective: of 109 ingredients across 50 "testosterone booster" products, only
24.8% had any data showing an increase, 10.1% had data showing a *decrease*, and 61.5% had no data at all
(Clemesha 2020, PMID 31496919-adjacent, *World J Mens Health* 2020;38(1):115-122). Thirteen products exceeded
safe upper limits for zinc, niacin or magnesium.

Safety notes that matter: ashwagandha has documented liver injury (Björnsson 2020) and several European
regulators have restricted it; tongkat ali products carried mercury above 0.5 ppm in 36 of 100 Malaysian
samples; one in five Ayurvedic products bought online contained lead, mercury or arsenic (Saper 2008).

## 5. Specifically for you

**You are probably eugonadal, and your main risks are self-inflicted.** Two things in your history point the
same way: years of high-volume endurance training (the exercise-hypogonadal literature describes a persistent
25–40% reduction in men with exactly your background), and Adderall-driven appetite suppression on top of a
high training load (the low-energy-availability literature describes much larger, fully reversible drops).
Both are central, hypothalamic and reversible, and neither is fixed by a supplement.

**Do this before anything else:**
1. **Get labs.** Morning (ideally 7–10 am), fasted, and **repeated** on a separate day before acting on
   anything — testosterone is pulsatile and a single low reading means little. Ask for: **total T, free T
   (equilibrium dialysis or calculated with SHBG), SHBG, LH, FSH, estradiol (sensitive assay), prolactin, TSH,
   25(OH)D, ferritin**, plus a CBC. LH is the one that decides which therapies could even apply: low T with
   normal or low LH is secondary (what clomiphene, enclomiphene and hCG treat); low T with high LH is
   testicular, and those drugs will not work.
2. **Fix the deficit problem.** Keep deficits moderate (~300–500 kcal/day), eat a real breakfast before the
   Adderall takes hold, drink calories midday, and make dinner big. See Nutrition → Eating on Adderall.
3. **Sleep 7–9 h.** Worth ~10–15% of your testosterone, and far more than that in lean-mass retention when
   you are cutting.
4. **If you are still running big weeks and want size, cut the volume.** That is the lever, not a pill.
5. **If labs come back genuinely low** (two morning draws under 300 ng/dL with symptoms), that is a doctor
   conversation, and the fertility-preserving options above exist precisely so you do not have to choose
   between treatment and sperm.

Ferritin is on the list for a different reason: it is commonly low in endurance athletes, it hurts training,
and very high values can mean iron overload, a recognised cause of low testosterone.

## 6. How Forge models each of these

Everything flows through one curve. `testosteroneEffect()` in `js/engine/metabolic.js` fits Bhasin's dose-response
data (1996 NEJM; 2001 AJP-Endo) — fat-free mass changes roughly with the log of serum T — and `modifiersAt()`
computes an **effective serum testosterone** from four sources, then feeds that single number through the same curve:

1. your **baseline** (a lab if you entered one; otherwise the age-average, reduced for short sleep and for a
   sustained calorie deficit);
2. **exogenous testosterone by any route** — injections, gel, oral undecanoate, nasal — which overrides everything
   else and suppresses the rest;
3. **endogenous therapies and boosters** (`kind: 'tEndogenous'` / `'tShift'`), combined by the interaction engine
   in §7, not added up;
4. a **measured on-therapy lab**, which always wins — Forge never adds a modeled rise on top of a real number.

Three rules keep the model honest:

- **Gates.** Each item declares who the evidence covers: `lowTOnly` (nothing for a man in the normal range),
  `secondaryOnly` (nothing if LH is already high), `obesityOnly` (BMI ≥ 30), `deficiencyOnly` (a measured low zinc),
  `highProlactin`, `shortSleepOnly`, `deficitOnly`. Fail a gate and the item contributes zero, with a sentence saying why.
- **Non-additivity** (§7): routes that share a final common path do not add up.
- **Muscle credit stops where the trials stopped** (§8): the flat 550 ng/dL ceiling is gone, replaced by a per-item,
  evidence-bounded credit.

## 7. The interaction model (CONTRACT §22)

`combineEndogenous()` in `js/engine/metabolic.js`. Three mechanisms, applied in order.

### 7.1 Cause-correction items go first
Fixing sleep, eating enough, losing fat if you are obese, repleting a measured zinc deficiency, normalizing a high
prolactin, cutting endurance volume — these change the **set-point**, not the drive. They are applied first,
multiplicatively (independent causes — an assumption), and because every drug's response shrinks as the baseline
rises, correcting a cause **reduces** what a drug can still add. That is real non-additivity, not a fudge:
clomiphene's `tPctByBaseline` curve falls from +91 % at 300 ng/dL to 0 at 450.

### 7.2 LH-driven items share one path, so the largest wins
SERMs (clomiphene, enclomiphene, tamoxifen), aromatase inhibitors, hCG and the herbals all end at the same place:
Leydig cells making testosterone. The only randomized test of the combination settles it.

**Habous 2018** (open-label RCT, 282 hypogonadal men wanting fertility, baseline ≈ 67 ng/dL, 12 weeks;
doi:10.1111/bju.14401, reported in Hohl 2025 doi:10.20945/2359-4292-2025-0093): clomiphene **+158**, hCG **+134**,
both together **+153 ng/dL**. The combination was **no better than clomiphene alone (p = 0.57)**, and the
three-arm comparison against hCG alone was significant for both the SERM and the SERM + hCG arms (p < 0.002).

So Forge combines LH-driven items as **the largest one wins** (`COMBO_BONUS = 0`, the measured point estimate), and
the optimistic end of the range adds 25 % of the others (`COMBO_BONUS_HI`, an **assumption** — the CI does not
exclude a small extra effect). Symptom scores did improve more on the combination in Habous, which Forge does not
model because it does not model symptoms.

### 7.3 Testicular capacity ceiling
Total LH-driven output is bounded by what the testes can make:

**Corrected 2026-10-20.** The old caps were 800 / 1,100, justified as "the highest group mean any SERM or AI trial
reached is 790 (Krause 1992)". That survey had only looked at hypogonadal trials plus Krause. The highest group
mean **any** own-axis trial has ever reported is **1,390 ng/dL** (48.2 ± 1.4 nmol/L) — Tenover & Bremner 1991,
five healthy men aged 26–33 on clomiphene 100 mg/day for 8 weeks. Both global ceilings are now 1,390 and they are
**equal**: nobody has shown hCG pushing a man past what a SERM has been measured to reach.

| Scope | Ceiling | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| `PITUITARY_CEILING` (fallback for SERMs / AIs) | **1,390 ng/dL** | Tenover & Bremner 1991, measured. **Caveat: n = 5, and 100 mg/day is 2–4× the usual clinical dose** |
| `TESTIS_CEILING` (absolute cap, all routes) | **1,390 ng/dL** | same. Forge does not extrapolate past a measurement |
| Clomiphene (`ceilingNg`) | **1,390** | Tenover 1991. At conventional doses: 612 at 1 year (Moskovic 2012), 610 on 25 mg (Shabsigh 2005) |
| Enclomiphene | **764** | Wiehle 2013, 25 mg, day-42 Cmax (morning C0hr 604). Was 800, a class-wide guess — now lowered |
| Tamoxifen | **790** | Krause 1992 |
| Aromatase inhibitor | 800 | unchanged. Mauras 2000 found +58 % in eugonadal males; the highest explicit AI group mean is 620 (Loves 2013), 572 (Leder 2004), 530 (Colleluori 2020), 525 (Burnett-Bowie 2009) |
| hCG | **1,100** | **assumption** (top of the male reference range). The highest level actually observed is ≈ 870 (Liu 2002, itself inferred) |

### 7.4 Primary hypogonadism attenuates the class — and only true primary failure blocks it
**Corrected 2026-10-20.** This used to be a cliff: LH > 9.4 IU/L and every item modeled zero. That was wrong in
two ways.

*Where 9.4 comes from.* Not, as the first pass said, the Kim 2016 enclomiphene enrolment criterion. It is the
**European Male Ageing Study** cut (Tajar 2010, JCEM 95:1810–18, doi:10.1210/jc.2009-1796) that defines the four
gonadal states: eugonadal (normal T, LH ≤ 9.4), secondary (low T, LH ≤ 9.4), **primary** (low T, LH > 9.4) and
**compensated** (normal T, LH > 9.4).

*What the response data say.* Keihani 2020 (n = 332) found **neither baseline LH nor FSH predicted the response**
and concluded that a "normal or slightly elevated" gonadotropin still responds; Patel 2015 (n = 47) and Roque 2026
(n = 60) agree. Mazzola 2014 (n = 76) and Kim 2026 (n = 292) do find higher LH predicts a worse response, but Kim's
is a **gradient — OR 0.82 per mIU/mL** — not a threshold. Nobody reports an LH at which the response is measured
to be zero.

*What Forge now does.*

| State | LH | Total T | Modeled |
|---|---|---|---|
| eugonadal / secondary | ≤ 9.4 | any | full response. LH is not consulted at all (Keihani 2020) |
| **compensated** | > 9.4 | ≥ 350 | **full response**, with a note. The testis is keeping up; this is not primary failure |
| **primary, mild** | 9.4 → 18.8 | < 350 | linear fade from 100 % to 0 %. At LH 14 that is ~51 % |
| **primary, clear** | ≥ 18.8 | < 350 | **zero**, with the reason |

`LH_PRIMARY_HARD = 18.8` (= 2 × 9.4) is an **assumption**: at that point Kim's odds ratio has compounded to
0.82^8.6 ≈ 0.18, about a fifth of the baseline odds of responding. It is not a measured cliff, and the doc says so.
The aromatase inhibitor still **blocks aromatase** whatever the gate says, so it still lowers estradiol and still
costs fat — correctly.

### 7.5 Exogenous testosterone suppresses everything
Any exogenous route — injections, gel, **oral undecanoate**, **nasal** — makes every own-axis item model zero.
Nasal testosterone keeps its distinct partial-preservation data as text (LH normal in 72.7 %, FSH in 81.8 %, sperm
above 5 M in 93.9 % at 6 months; Ramasamy 2020, single-arm, 6 months, heavy attrition), not as a modeled difference.

### 7.6 Estradiol, SHBG and free testosterone
Each item declares `e2Pct` (serum estradiol), `e2ActionPct` (estrogen **action**) and `shbgDelta`. Free testosterone
is then computed from the combined total T and SHBG with the **Vermeulen** equations, which the app already used.

| Item | Estradiol | SHBG | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clomiphene / enclomiphene | **+34 pg/mL** pooled (95 % CI 19–49, I² 81 %); directly measured in eugonadal men at **37 → 54 pg/mL** (Bremner 1987) and **24 → 86 pg/mL** (Tenover 1991) | **+3.63 nmol/L, not significant** (CI −0.54 to 7.81, p = 0.09); +28 % at 7 days and **no change at 8 weeks** in healthy men | Hohl 2025, Bremner 1987, Tenover 1991. Free T rose (SMD 1.57, CI 0.44–2.70) and DHT +7.58 ng/dL |
| Aromatase inhibitor | **−35 %** (mean of the measured values) | 0 (Mauras 2000) | −24 % and −20 % (Burnett-Bowie 2009 ×2), −50 % (Loves 2013), −48 % (Mauras 2000), −52 % (Colleluori 2020) |
| hCG | **+150 %**, in step with testosterone | 0 | Liu 2002 |
| Fat loss (obese) | −25 % | +6 | Corona 2013 |

**Muscle credit is driven by FREE testosterone, not total (added 2026-10-20).** `combineEndogenous` computes
`freeAdj = (freeT / freeTBase) / (totalT / T0)` — the fraction of the total-T rise that survives into free T —
and shrinks the credited level by it. It is **capped at 1**, so saturable SHBG binding can only ever take credit
away, never add it. With the measured +4 nmol/L SHBG rise the factor comes out at ≈1.0, i.e. the correction does
essentially nothing. **That is the finding, not a fudge**: see §2's free-testosterone paragraph, where feeding
Bremner 1987's measured numbers through the same Vermeulen routine reproduces his measured 65 % → 66 %
bioavailable fraction. The machinery is there because it *does* bite for a man with a high SHBG or a larger
modeled SHBG rise (a +15 nmol/L rise costs ~10 % of the credit), and because the question deserved a number
rather than a hand-wave.

**A SERM's serum-E2 rise is never turned into fat loss.** Finkelstein's finding is about estrogen *action*, and a
SERM raises the hormone while blocking the receptor in some tissues; the direction in adipose is unknown, so it is
modeled as zero and said out loud.

### 7.7 Estradiol → fat: the Finkelstein term
**Finkelstein 2013** (NEJM 369:1011, doi:10.1056/NEJMoa1206168; n = 400, goserelin + graded testosterone gel ±
anastrozole, 16 weeks). With testosterone dose held constant, suppressing estradiol raised fat and did nothing to
muscle:

| Outcome | Cohort × dose interaction | Between-cohort, among men given testosterone |
|---|---|---|
| % body fat | **P = 0.001** | **P < 0.001** |
| Subcutaneous fat area | P = 0.029 | P < 0.001 |
| Intra-abdominal fat area | P = 0.021 | P = 0.002 |
| Total-body lean mass | **P = 0.22 (ns)** | P = 0.52 (ns) |
| Thigh-muscle area | P = 0.20 (ns) | P = 0.19 (ns) |
| Leg-press strength | **P = 0.91 (ns)** | P = 0.90 (ns) |

Hormone levels: cohort 1 (no AI) testosterone 44 / 191 / 337 / 470 / 805 ng/dL with estradiol 3.6 / 7.9 / 11.9 /
18.2 / 33.3 pg/mL; cohort 2 (anastrozole) testosterone 41 / 231 / 367 / 485 / 924 with estradiol 1.0 / 1.2 / 2.0 /
2.1 / 2.8. In cohort 2, % body fat rose by a **similar amount at every dose**, which is what identifies the effect
as estrogenic rather than androgenic.

**The per-arm kilogram magnitudes exist only in that paper's figures.** Every number in Forge's fat term is
therefore an assumption (§9): near-total blockade (≈ 90 % loss of estrogen action, which is what cohort 2 had)
costs **1.5 kg of fat over 16 weeks**, range 0.5–3.0 kg, scaled linearly with the fraction of estrogen action
actually lost and following the same saturating time course as the testosterone term.

**That scaling is what reconciles Finkelstein with every real-world AI trial.** Finkelstein's testes were switched
off, so anastrozole had almost no substrate and estradiol crashed by ~90 %. In a man with a working axis the same
1 mg only drops estradiol 20–50 %, because the rising testosterone feeds the enzyme more substrate. So the modeled
penalty there is a few tenths of a kilogram — which is exactly why:

- **Burnett-Bowie 2009** (RCT, 12 months): body composition (CT and DXA) and strength did not change.
- **Burnett-Bowie 2009b** (RCT, 12 months): spine BMD fell 1.121 → 1.102 g/cm² on anastrozole while rising on
  placebo (P = 0.0014). "Aromatase inhibition does not improve skeletal health in aging men."
- **Loves 2013** (RCT, letrozole, BMI > 35, 6 months): testosterone 248 → 620 ng/dL and "significant effects on the
  predefined endpoints were not observed".
- **Mauras 2000** (anastrozole 1 mg, 10 weeks, males 15–22, estradiol −48 %): no change in fat mass, fat-free mass,
  protein synthesis, muscle strength or calcium kinetics; testosterone +58 %, SHBG unchanged.
- **Dias 2016 / 2017** (RCT, 12 months, men ≥ 65): the anastrozole arm **lost** abdominal subcutaneous fat
  (−60.3 ± 3.2 cm², p = 0.003) and leptin (−1.5 ± 1.2 ng/mL, p = 0.04).

**Sign-reversal gate — Colleluori 2020** (doi:10.3389/fendo.2020.00277; randomized, double-blind, 23 men, BMI ≥ 35,
total T < 300, estradiol ≥ 10.9, LH < 9, anastrozole 1 mg + weight loss vs placebo + weight loss, 6 months).
Testosterone 530 ± 166 vs 290 ± 133 ng/dL (p = 0.01); estradiol 14.0 ± 10.6 vs 29.1 ± 7.9 (p = 0.01); fat mass
**−4.4 ± 3.9 vs −0.7 ± 1.9 kg** (adjusted p = 0.04); no difference in lean mass or in knee strength; LDL +5.7 vs
−30.9 mg/dL (p = 0.03).

Forge switches the fat **penalty off** in that population (BMI ≥ 35 with estradiol ≥ 25 pg/mL) and deliberately does
**not** model a benefit, because the AI arm also lost **5.1 kg more weight** (−8.3 vs −3.2 kg, adjusted p = 0.17,
not significant), which accounts for essentially all of the fat difference. The trial's raw direction is reported as
the optimistic end of the range.

## 8. The muscle-credit ceiling: what replaced `T_NORMAL_MID = 550`

The old model credited muscle up to a flat 550 ng/dL (with per-item overrides). That constant is **gone**. Muscle
credit now follows achieved serum T on the Bhasin curve and is bounded per item by two things: whether that item's
own trials measured body composition at all, and what level they reached.

| Class | Meaning | What Forge does |
|---|---|---|
| **measured** | trials of this item measured body composition **in men like you** | credit the achieved level, bounded at `bcMaxLevel` = where those trials landed |
| **extrapolated** | no body-composition data, but a measured T rise | run it through the Bhasin T→lean curve, bounded by the highest serum T this item's own trials reached, and **label it an extrapolation** everywhere it is shown; the lower end of its range is **zero**, because zero is what has been measured |
| **none** | trials measured body composition and found none | credit **nothing** (`tLeanShare: 0`), and apply the estradiol fat term where it applies |

| Item | Class | `bcMaxLevel` | Where the bound comes from |
|---|---|---|---|
| hCG | measured, for baselines **≤ 433** (`bcMaxBaseline`) | **870** | Liu 2002: lean +2 kg (p < 0.001), fat −1 kg (p < 0.05), and **no** change in shoulder or knee strength, gait, balance or activity. Entry ≤ 15 nmol/L (433 ng/dL) with a reported +150 % → ≈ 870. The achieved mean is not published, so the bound is an **assumption**. A man whose baseline is already above 433 is not that population, so his credit is flagged extrapolated |
| Clomiphene | measured **below**, extrapolated **above** | **500** measured, **1,390** extrapolated (`bcExtrapMaxLevel`), measured only for baselines **≤ 300** (`bcMaxBaseline`) | Soares 2018: lean (p < 0.001), fat-free (p = 0.004) and muscle mass (p < 0.001) improved **by bioimpedance** in obese men starting at 226 ± 70 ng/dL with an entry criterion of T ≤ 300. The achieved level is not in the abstract; 500 is inferred from that baseline plus the pooled clomiphene rise in obese men (+333 ng/dL, Tienforti 2023) and is an **assumption**. Above 500, and for any man whose own baseline is already above 300, the credit is **extrapolated** and bounded by the level the hormone trials reached (1,390) |
| Enclomiphene | extrapolated | **604** | no enclomiphene trial has ever measured body composition. 604 ± 160 ng/dL is the highest on-treatment group mean (Wiehle 2013, 25 mg, day 42) |
| Tamoxifen | extrapolated | **790** | nobody has measured body composition on tamoxifen in men. Krause 1992 is the only randomized rise in **eugonadal** men (490 → 790 vs 530 → 560 on placebo) |
| Aromatase inhibitor | **none** | — | measured and null in four trials (above) |
| Ashwagandha | extrapolated | **726** | Wankhede 2015 measured body composition but its end testosterone did not differ from placebo, so the body-composition result cannot be attributed to the hormone. 726 is the highest on-treatment mean |
| Tongkat ali | extrapolated | **350** | Chinnappan 2021's men stayed below 300; the meta-analysis was only significant below 300 |
| Zinc repletion | extrapolated | **500** | Prasad 1996 moved marginally deficient men 239 → 461 ng/dL; no body composition measured |
| Cabergoline | extrapolated | **700** | Colao 2004 measured bone density, not body composition; 61 % normalized, 39 % still needed replacement |
| Eating enough | measured | 1,100 | Murphy & Koehler 2021: a 500 kcal/day deficit abolished lean-mass gain from lifting |
| Sleep 7–9 h | measured | 900 | Nedeltcheva 2010: 5.5 h vs 8.5 h shifted diet weight loss from fat to lean |
| Fat loss (obese) | measured | 700 | Corona 2013; the fat loss itself is what the projection simulates |

The bound is **composed the way the level is**: the cause-correction chain is bounded by the cause items' own
evidence, and whatever a drug adds on top is bounded by that **drug's** bound. A lifestyle item with a generous
bound cannot launder a drug's rise past the drug's own evidence — fixing sleep and taking clomiphene still credits
muscle only to 500 ng/dL.

**Two further rules decide `extrapolated`, added 2026-10-20.** A row is extrapolated when (1) the item has no
body-composition data at all, **or (2) the man's own baseline is above the highest baseline that trial enrolled**
(`bcMaxBaseline`: Soares took T ≤ 300, Liu took ≤ 433), **or (3) more than half the credited rise sits above
`bcMaxLevel`**. Rule 2 is the one that matters for Bob: clomiphene's body composition was measured in obese
hypogonadal men, and a eugonadal man is simply not that population however good the hormone data are. A small
overshoot of a bound that is itself inferred is treated as noise, which is why a hypogonadal man landing at
~573 ng/dL stays `measured`.

**For a man already mid-normal, the hormone moves and the muscle is unproven — and the honesty has moved with
it.** The old model made clomiphene, enclomiphene and hCG return exactly zero above 450–500 ng/dL, which hid the
real result behind a wrong one. They now show their modeled rise (Bob: 548 → 823 on clomiphene), a lean number,
and an **Extrapolated** badge with a range starting at zero, so the planner still ranks them at zero. The
aromatase inhibitor remains the sharpest case: it *does* raise a eugonadal man's testosterone (Mauras 2000:
+58 %), Forge models that rise, and credits zero muscle plus a fat cost, because four trials measured it.

### 8b. The open question Forge will not paper over
Every lean number above comes from the **Bhasin 2001** testosterone→lean dose-response. That was built by giving
men graded doses of testosterone **enanthate** while suppressing their gonadotropins with a GnRH agonist. A SERM
does the opposite: it drives the man's own Leydig cells, raises LH and FSH, raises estradiol by 30–60 pg/mL, and
nudges SHBG up. **Nobody has ever tested whether an endogenous rise of a given size builds the same muscle as an
exogenous rise of that size.** The two attempts in this whole class are both in hypogonadal men and both carry a
warning:
- **Soares 2018** (clomiphene, obese men, T ≤ 300): lean, fat-free and muscle mass up — by **bioimpedance**, not DXA.
- **Liu 2002** (hCG, men > 60, T ≤ 433): lean **+2 kg**, and **no** improvement in shoulder strength, knee
  strength, gait, balance or physical activity. More mass on the scan did not become more function.

Searching PubMed for any SERM body-composition trial in men turns up nothing else: `clomiphene[Title] AND (body
composition OR lean mass OR muscle)[Title]` returns a chicken-residue study, an IVF-offspring letter and a
guinea-pig ileum paper. Tamoxifen has never been measured on body composition in men at all.

So the second half of Bob's hypothesis — *"bet there are hypertrophic effects to anyone"* — is **not supported
and not refuted; it is untested**. Forge models it, labels it extrapolated, ranks it at zero, and says this in
the catalogue entry, in the planner's group heading and in the per-row note.

## 10d. The eugonadal correction (2026-10-20, second pass)

Bob: *"The clomiphene thing — I bet that works for anyone, bet there are hypertrophic effects to anyone, and we
should model that."* He is **right on the hormone and the question is open on the muscle**. What changed:

| | Before | After | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clomiphene `tPctByBaseline` | zero at **450 ng/dL** | constant **+274 ng/dL** to 550, tapering to ≈ +220 by 1,000 | Tenover 1991 (519 → 1,390), Bremner 1987 (490 → 980), Krause 1992 (490 → 790). The zero was an extrapolation of a hypogonadal fit, never a measurement |
| Enclomiphene | zero at 450 | constant **+216 ng/dL** on the same shape | Same correction. The eugonadal arm is inferred from clomiphene (×216/274) — **assumption A15** |
| Tamoxifen | flat **+60 %** at every baseline | constant **+300 ng/dL** (= +61 % at Krause's 490) | A flat percentage implied a bigger absolute rise the higher you start, which nothing supports |
| hCG | zero at **500 ng/dL** | tapering curve to 1,100 | hCG stimulation is the standard Leydig-reserve test *in normal men* (Cailleux-Bounacer 2008, Panidis 2002). Single-dose only, so the arm is **assumption A16** |
| Cabergoline | hard zero at 450 | smooth taper to zero at **700** | The taper is right — it gives back what prolactin took — but the cliff at 450 was arbitrary. `highProlactin` stays: that one *is* what Colao 2004 shows |
| `secondaryOnly` gate | **zero** whenever LH > 9.4 | zero only at LH ≥ 18.8 **with** T < 350; linear fade from 9.4; **no effect at all** on a man with a normal T | Keihani 2020 (n = 332): LH did not predict response, and "normal or slightly elevated" still responds. Kim 2026: a gradient (OR 0.82/mIU/mL), not a cliff. EMAS defines "compensated" (raised LH, normal T) as a separate state |
| `PITUITARY_CEILING` / `TESTIS_CEILING` | 800 / 1,100 | **1,390 / 1,390** | Tenover 1991's measured group mean. Per-item: clomiphene 1,390, enclomiphene **764** (lowered from 800), tamoxifen **790** |
| Muscle credit | total T, flat per-item cap | **free T**, with a measured bound and a separate extrapolated bound | `freeAdj` (§7.6). The free-T objection was checked and does not bite — the bioavailable fraction does not fall on a SERM |
| `extrapolated` | a per-item class | a **per-man** judgement (`bcMaxBaseline`, half-the-rise rule) | A eugonadal man is outside Soares 2018's population even though clomiphene's class says `measured` |

**Deliberately left alone**, because the gate is genuinely what the trials show:
- **Cabergoline's `highProlactin`** — Colao 2004 treated prolactinoma; a normal prolactin has nothing to treat.
- **Vitamin D's `deficiencyOnly`** and **zinc's** (serum zinc < 70 µg/dL) — supplementing replete men did nothing.
- **Tongkat ali's zero in the normal range** (`tPct: 0`, `tPctLowT: 15`) — the meta-analysis subgroup above
  300 ng/dL was not significant. That is a measured null, not an extrapolation.
- **Ashwagandha** already had no baseline gate (+12 % at any level) and keeps it.
- **The aromatase inhibitors' `bodyComp: 'none'`** — four trials measured body composition on them and found none.
- **`energy_availability`, `sleep_hygiene`, `fat_loss`** — their gates are "you must actually have the problem",
  which is exactly what the trials studied.

**What was looked for and not found.** A PubMed search for any SERM body-composition trial in men other than
Soares 2018 returns nothing (`clomiphene[Title] AND (body composition OR lean mass OR muscle)[Title]` → a
chicken-residue study, an IVF-offspring letter, a guinea-pig ileum paper). No athlete, bodybuilder or
post-cycle study with real before/after measurements in eugonadal men turned up either. No within-trial
clomiphene dose ladder exists in men.

## 10c. The planner (`js/engine/tplan.js`)

Ranks every single item and a curated set of combinations by **modeled 12-month lean and fat change**, not by the
size of the testosterone rise — because those are different lists, and the aromatase inhibitor is the proof.

- **Input**: his labs where present; population defaults for his age where not, each one flagged "assumed". When
  the decisive labs (total T and LH) are missing, "get the panel" is the first and highest-value action, with the
  exact list and why each one matters (`T_LAB_PANEL`).
- **Output per row**: modeled total T, free T (Vermeulen) and estradiol with ranges; the projected lean and fat
  delta computed through the same `project()` the Projection screen uses, so adding the row changes Projection by
  exactly what the row said; evidence grade; the measured/extrapolated/none class; who it suits and who it does
  not; the labs to monitor; fertility; and, for prescription items, that they need a prescriber.
- **Honesty**: an option modeled at ~0 is shown as ~0 with the reason, not omitted and not padded. Lifestyle levers
  rank in the same list on their own merits.
- **One tap** adds the item or the whole combination to the stack; a second tap removes it.
- **Anti-doping**: SERMs and other anti-estrogens are WADA-prohibited at all times under **S4.2**, aromatase
  inhibitors under **S4.1**, hCG in males under **S2.2**, testosterone by any route under **S1**.
- **Women** (§20): the planner returns `{ hidden: true }` and no rows, and the UI does not render it.

## 12. The isomer question: is enclomiphene better tolerated, with less estrogen effect? (2026-10-20)

Bob: *"I hear enclomiphene is better tolerated and has less of the estrogen effects. Study that, implement it."*

**Short answer: half right, and the half that is right is the half people usually get backwards.** "Better
tolerated" is supported, on one study. "Less of the estrogen effects" is supported for the *effects* and
**contradicted for serum estradiol**, which goes up on enclomiphene about as much as on clomiphene.

### 12.1 The pharmacology is real, and it is bigger than it looks

Clomiphene citrate is a mixture of two geometric isomers: **62 % (trans)enclomiphene** and **38 %
(cis)zuclomiphene** (Wiehle 2013, doi:10.1111/bju.12363). Enclomiphene is the estrogen antagonist that raises
LH, FSH and testosterone. Zuclomiphene is an estrogen **agonist** that contributes little to the testosterone
rise, and its half-life is about **30 days** against enclomiphene's **~10 h** (Rodriguez 2016,
doi:10.1080/14656566.2016.1204294), so it accumulates.

That accumulation has been measured, and it is dramatic. In 15 men on clomiphene 25 mg/day for at least six
weeks, median serum **zuclomiphene was 44.0 ng/mL against 2.2 ng/mL of enclomiphene — a 20:1 ratio** (Helo
2017, doi:10.1111/bju.13625). Age, BMI, treatment duration and testosterone level did not predict either
concentration. So after a month or two on clomiphene, **almost all the drug in circulation is the estrogenic
isomer**, and the one doing the work is a rounding error. The authors' own conclusion was that this "supports
the need for the development of a pure selective oestrogen receptor antagonist".

Supporting, in a species rather than a test tube: in baboons given the isomers separately for 12 days,
zuclomiphene did **not** significantly raise testosterone and raised serum cholesterol 22 %, while enclomiphene
took testosterone 170 → 1,144 ng/dL (clomiphene itself 170 → 559) and **lowered** cholesterol 8 % (Rodriguez
2016, reviewing the Repros preclinical programme).

So the mechanistic case for Bob's claim is strong. The clinical case is thinner.

### 12.2 Tolerability: supported, on one study

**There has never been a randomized trial of enclomiphene against clomiphene.** The entire direct comparison in
the literature is one retrospective chart review.

**Saffati 2024** (Transl Androl Urol 13(9):1984–1990, doi:10.21037/tau-24-238; Baylor, 66 men, mean age 38.5,
BMI 30.2, 78 % secondary hypogonadism). Each man took clomiphene first (median 18.7 months) and was then
switched to enclomiphene (median 8.9 months), serving as his own control.

| Side effect | Clomiphene | Enclomiphene | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Any adverse event** | **47 % (31/66)** | **13.8 % (8/58)** | **0.001** (OR 0.18, 95 % CI 0.07–0.44) |
| Decreased libido | 33.3 % (22/66) | 8.6 % (5/58) | 0.001 (OR 0.18, CI 0.06–0.53) |
| Erectile dysfunction | 18.2 % (12/66) | 8.6 % (5/58) | 0.12 — ns |
| Fatigue | 18.2 % (12/66) | 6.9 % (4/58) | 0.06 — ns |
| Decreased energy | 16.7 % (11/66) | 5.2 % (3/58) | 0.044 |
| Mood changes | 9.1 % (6/66) | 0 % (0/58) | 0.03 |
| Depressive thoughts | 4.5 % (3/66) | 0 % (0/58) | 0.24 — ns |
| Gynecomastia | 3.0 % (2/66) | 0 % (0/58) | 0.49 — ns |
| Agitation | 6.1 % (4/66) | 1.7 % (1/58) | 0.37 — ns |
| Hematocrit change (median) | +0.4 % | 0 % | 0.85 — ns |

**Why this is not proof,** mostly in the authors' own words: retrospective, single institution, unblinded,
adverse events counted from whether a clinician wrote them in a note with a diagnosis code; **no washout**
between the two drugs, with zuclomiphene's ~30-day half-life sitting right in the middle of that problem;
everyone took the drugs in the same order, so a period effect and an ascertainment effect both point the same
way (a second drug's side effects are simply less likely to get written down); men switched for different
reasons; and the baselines differ — clomiphene started in a hypogonadal state (306 ng/dL) while enclomiphene
started from an already-corrected one (436 ng/dL). Three of the differences (gynecomastia, depressive thoughts,
agitation) are a handful of events and are not significant.

**What the randomized evidence says** is a weaker but cleaner claim: enclomiphene is no worse than placebo.
- **Kim 2016** (two parallel phase-3 RCTs, 256 men): 53 men (21 %) had an adverse event attributed to study
  drug, **none severe**, and **no significant difference between enclomiphene, testosterone gel and placebo**.
  *This 21 % is the rate across all arms, including placebo — the previous Forge entry reported it as an
  enclomiphene-specific rate, which was wrong, and it is now corrected.* Discontinuations: 2 in the 25 mg arm
  (one raised hematocrit/haemoglobin, one raised PSA), 1 on gel (hematocrit), 8 in the placebo/gel arms overall.
- **Wiehle 2014** (phase 2b, 124 men): treatment-emergent AEs in 9–20 % of subjects, similar across all arms;
  the only enclomiphene events were one mildly raised estradiol and two headaches; **no discontinuations for an
  adverse event and no serious AEs on enclomiphene**.

**Clomiphene's own safety record is also good on hard endpoints**, which is worth saying because "better
tolerated" is sometimes heard as "clomiphene is dangerous". It is not:
- **Krzastek 2019** (doi:10.1097/JU.0000000000000396, 400 men, mean 25.5 months, up to 7 years): among the 120
  treated beyond 3 years, 88 % reached eugonadism, 77 % reported improved symptoms and **8 % reported a side
  effect** — mood changes in 5, blurred vision in 3, breast tenderness in 2. **No significant adverse event in
  any of the 400 patients.**
- **Guo 2019** (doi:10.5534/wjmh.190028, 12,318 US men in MarketScan claims; 5,118 with adequate follow-up):
  **no increase** in thrombotic events, vision problems, liver disease or nausea in the 6 months after starting
  versus the 6 months before. Gynecomastia, mental disorders, skin disorders and hypertension were coded *less*
  often afterwards, on low event counts.
- **Soares 2018** (doi:10.1038/s41366-018-0105-2): **no** adverse-event withdrawals in the clomiphene arm (both
  withdrawals were in the placebo arm); hematocrit unchanged (p = 0.409); PSA rose 0.62 → 0.76 ng/mL (p = 0.023)
  but stayed in range; somnolence in 5/39 vs 0/39 on placebo (p = 0.064).
- **VTE** is case-report-level only in men (an isolated cortical vein thrombosis, Choi 2020
  doi:10.1007/s10072-020-04862-z; a thrombotic STEMI in a man in his early 30s, Shahid 2025
  doi:10.1177/00368504251349392) with no excess in Guo's 5,118-man analysis. Tamoxifen is the SERM here with a
  labelled VTE risk, and nobody has quantified it in men using it this way.
- **Bone density** has never been measured on either SERM in men. It *has* been measured on an aromatase
  inhibitor, where spine BMD fell over 12 randomized months (Burnett-Bowie 2009) — which is why the AI is the
  one item in this group Forge grades `poor`.

**Lipids are the one place the isomer story shows up in a hard number.** Clomiphene **lowered HDL, p < 0.001**
(Soares 2018). Enclomiphene did not move HDL, LDL, total cholesterol or triglycerides at 6.25, 12.5 or 25 mg
over 6 weeks (Wiehle 2013, across-group p = 0.78 for HDL). With the baboon cholesterol result pointing the same
way, that is a consistent, mechanistically coherent, non-self-reported difference.

**The one finding against enclomiphene:** IGF-1 fell on every dose, ≈96 → 50–62 µg/L over 6 weeks, significantly
more than on gel (p ≤ 0.008 for all three doses; Wiehle 2013). Levels stayed inside the reference range and
nobody knows what it means — but for someone whose goal is muscle it is the result in this section that should
give the most pause, and it is in the catalogue's risk list for that reason.

**Verdict: PARTLY SUPPORTED.** Direction is consistent across one direct comparison, the lipid data and the
preclinical isomer work; the magnitude rests on a single unblinded retrospective study. Forge grades
enclomiphene `good` and clomiphene `moderate`, and the grade is capped there — not raised to "proven" — by the
design of the only study that compares them.

### 12.3 Serum estradiol: NOT supported, and this is the important correction

Forge used to model enclomiphene's estradiol rise at **half** clomiphene's (`e2Pct: 45` vs `90`) on the
reasoning that the estrogenic isomer had been removed. **That reasoning conflates two different things.**

Serum estradiol rises on a SERM because the SERM raises testosterone, and testosterone is the substrate
aromatase works on. Removing zuclomiphene does nothing to aromatase. The data:

| | Enclomiphene | Clomiphene | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Serum E2, before → after | **29.5 → 67 pg/mL** (p = 0.003, n = 18) | 31 → 37.6 (p = 0.074, n = 11) | Thomas 2023, doi:10.7759/cureus.41476 |
| Mean change, between groups | +61.2 pg/mL | +25.0 | same, p = 0.164 — ns |
| T/E ratio | 13.2 → **8.7** (worse) | 7.9 → **12.5** (better) | same |
| Change from an on-clomiphene baseline | −5.92 pg/mL | +17.5 | Saffati 2024, p = 0.001 |
| Pooled SERM vs placebo (both isomers) | +33.99 pg/mL (CI 19.19–48.79) | | Hohl 2025 |

Thomas 2023 went looking for exactly Bob's hypothesis and found the opposite: *"Theoretically, EC has been
proposed to cause less estrogenic effects than CC… However, our results showed that the T/E ratio considerably
decreased in men who received EC, while it significantly improved in men who received CC."* Hohl's pooled
+34 pg/mL includes the enclomiphene trials. Wiehle 2014 lists raised estradiol as an enclomiphene adverse event.

**Saffati's −5.92 does not contradict any of this**, and it is the number people quote. It is a change measured
from a level clomiphene had *already* raised, not from an untreated baseline — stopping the isomer that was
raising estrogen action lowers it relative to where clomiphene left you. It says nothing about what enclomiphene
does from zero.

The two direct studies therefore point in opposite directions on serum E2, both on tiny estradiol sub-samples
(n = 18 and 11 in Thomas), with channelling bias in both: 6 of Thomas's 46 enclomiphene patients had already
quit clomiphene for side effects, while every one of Saffati's enclomiphene patients was a clomiphene user first.

**Forge's correction:** `e2Pct` 45 → **85**, just under clomiphene's 90 in proportion to the slightly smaller
pooled testosterone rise. The modeled estradiol for the two drugs is now within ~3 %. The entry says in words
that estradiol goes **up**, because the number alone will be read the other way.

**The distinction that survives** is the one Forge already makes for aromatase inhibitors: *serum estradiol* is
not *estrogen action*. Zuclomiphene is a long-lived estrogen agonist at 20× the concentration of its partner;
removing it lowers estrogen **action** in tissue while leaving the **hormone** where it was. That is a coherent
explanation for Saffati's side-effect table and for the HDL difference, and it is why Forge treats "fewer
estrogen-mediated side effects" as supported while treating "lower estradiol" as refuted. Neither SERM feeds the
Finkelstein fat term, which is driven by estrogen action being *blocked*; a SERM's direction there is unknown,
so it stays at zero for both.

### 12.4 Efficacy: no significant difference in either direct comparison

| Comparison | Enclomiphene | Clomiphene | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tienforti 2023 pooled, obesity-related, n = 292 (isomer-matched, **not** circular) | +7.50 nmol/L = **+216 ng/dL** (CI 188–245, I² = 4 %) | +11.56 nmol/L = **+333** (CI 9.68–13.43, I² = 69 %) | ratio **0.65** |
| Thomas 2023, 46 vs 32, ≥3 months | +124.96 ng/dL (390 → 581) | +177.30 (244 → 470) | **0.491 — ns** (ratio 0.70) |
| Saffati 2024, 66 men, sequential | +166 ng/dL (436 → 499) | +98 (306 → 436) | **0.20 — ns** (enclomiphene higher) |
| vs testosterone gel (both pooled, Hohl 2025) | difference 5.41 ng/dL (CI −43 to +54) | | 0.83 — ns |

So the isomer-matched meta-analysis says enclomiphene is about two-thirds as strong, and neither head-to-head
can distinguish them. Forge's curve sits at **0.787 ×** clomiphene's at every baseline — between those readings
— and the disagreement is carried in the confidence interval (`tCI` widened from [0.87, 1.13] to **[0.80,
1.45]**) rather than hidden inside the point estimate.

Gonadotropins are where enclomiphene wins consistently: Thomas found LH and FSH rose significantly on
enclomiphene and not on clomiphene, and total motile sperm count improved only in the enclomiphene group.

**The dose-response** is now modeled from Wiehle 2013 rather than treated as flat. Measured as a rise over each
arm's own baseline: morning C0 gives 6.25 mg = 0.44 and 12.5 mg = 0.31 of the 25 mg arm; the 24-h mean gives
0.45 and 0.51. The two lower arms are indistinguishable (n = 12 and 7, across-group p = 0.07 for C0), so Forge
models both at **0.43** and puts the step at the top dose — `response: [[0,0],[25,0.43],[50,0.43],[100,1]]`,
expressed as a share of the studied regimen rather than in mg, like every other §22 prescription item, so the
model never prints a number a man could dose himself from. Above the top dose it plateaus: 50 mg was given for
two weeks in one arm and never in the 3-month programme, and Wiehle's own reading was that the limit is the
testis, not the dose — tripling LH from 6.7 to 13.3 IU/L raised the 24-h mean testosterone only 27 %,
*"suggesting a dose-limiting effect on the testosterone-producing Leydig cells… and the inability of
enclomiphene citrate to stimulate the production of supernormal levels of testosterone."*

### 12.5 What replaced assumption A15

**The old A15 was wrong in its own description, not just uncertain.** It read: *"enclomiphene's entire eugonadal
arm = clomiphene's eugonadal response × 216/274."* Two problems:

1. **It was circular.** 274 ng/dL is Hohl 2025's pooled **SERM** effect, and that pool contains the enclomiphene
   trials (Kim 2016 and both Wiehle 2014 studies) alongside the clomiphene ones. Scaling enclomiphene against a
   pool that includes enclomiphene is not a comparison. (Forge's clomiphene entry is now corrected to say that
   +274 is a class estimate, not a clomiphene-specific one; the clomiphene-specific figure is Tienforti's +333,
   and Forge keeps 274 as the deliberately conservative anchor.)
2. **It misdescribed the arithmetic.** The anchors are 0.787 × clomiphene's at every baseline, and 0.787 × 274 =
   216 — which is simply Tienforti's directly measured, **enclomiphene-specific** pooled effect. The hypogonadal
   arm of that curve is a *measurement of this drug*, not an inference from the other one.

**Restated A15: what is inferred is the SHAPE above ~350 ng/dL, not the size.** Forge carries a constant
absolute rise upward because Keihani 2020 (n = 332) and Kim 2026 (n = 292) both found baseline total T does not
predict the size of the response — and **both of those are clomiphene cohorts.** Nobody has tested whether
enclomiphene behaves the same way, because nobody has given it to a eugonadal man. That is the assumption, and
it is now a much smaller and more precisely stated one.

**Ceiling, re-derived (A15b).** 764 ng/dL is Wiehle 2013's 25 mg day-42 **24-h Cmax** — the highest group mean
the drug has produced by any measure. The highest **morning** group mean (C0, which is the measurement Forge's
serum numbers actually are) is 604, and the highest 24-h mean is 586. Comparing a morning level to a Cmax is the
generous reading, and the obvious fix would be to drop the ceiling to 604. **Rejected, deliberately:** 604 is
the highest level observed in obese *hypogonadal* men at the only dose anyone pushed to, not a probe of the
drug's ceiling. Clomiphene's 1,390 *is* a probe — Tenover 1991 gave it to healthy eugonadal men. Hard-capping
enclomiphene at 604 would open a ~700 ng/dL gap between two drugs that are the same molecule minus an isomer
that barely moves testosterone, which would be an artefact of who got enrolled rather than a pharmacological
claim. The conservative reading is carried where it belongs: `bcMaxLevel` stays at **604** as a hard bound on
*muscle credit*, and every extrapolated row's lean range still starts at zero.

**`bcMaxLevel` 604, re-derived.** Unchanged, but it means something different here than for clomiphene. For
clomiphene, 500 is "where the body-composition trial landed". For enclomiphene **no body-composition trial
exists at all**, so 604 is simply "the highest level this drug has ever been shown to produce", and Forge
refuses to run the muscle curve above it. That is now said explicitly in `bcNote`.

### 12.6 Tolerability as a modeled property (CONTRACT §22.8)

Bob's question had no field to land in: the catalogue could describe side effects in prose but the planner could
not rank on them and nothing forced a rate to carry its denominator. Every §22 prescription item now carries
`model.tolerability` with a grade, **the comparator the grade is relative to**, a discontinuation rate with its
denominator, itemised events each with `n of N` and the comparator arm where one exists, the p-value (including
"not significant", explicitly), the caveats, and its own citations.

| Item | Grade | On what |
|---|---|---|
| Enclomiphene | **good** | No worse than placebo in 2 phase-3 RCTs + 1 phase-2b; 13.8 % vs 47 % against clomiphene in the one head-to-head. Capped at `good` because that head-to-head is unblinded and retrospective |
| Cabergoline | **good** | ~4.5 % side effects over 24 months, and the only item here FDA-approved for the condition it treats |
| Clomiphene | **moderate** | Excellent on hard endpoints (400 men to 7 years, 12,318 in claims, no excess thrombosis/vision/liver); common on symptoms (33 % low libido in the head-to-head); HDL falls, p < 0.001 |
| hCG | **moderate** | No AE table has ever been published; graded on the two things that *were* measured — testis volume −5 mL and estradiol +150 % — plus being an injection |
| Aromatase inhibitor | **poor** | Spine BMD fell over 12 randomized months. Graded on a scan, not on symptoms: symptom scores actually matched clomiphene (p = 0.634) |
| Tamoxifen | **unknown** | No trial has tabulated AEs for this use in men. Grading it `good` because nothing bad was reported would be reading silence as safety |

Rules enforced by `test/supp-tolerability.test.js`: no rate without a denominator; no grade without a
comparator; a non-significant difference must be labelled non-significant; and a combination inherits the
**worst** grade among its items. The planner uses the grade only as a **late tiebreak**, after modeled lean/fat
and evidence grade — which is exactly where Bob's question bites, because at a normal baseline clomiphene and
enclomiphene score identically (zero, both extrapolated) on the same evidence grade, so before this they were
ordered alphabetically and now the better-tolerated one leads.

*(That schema rule immediately caught a real data problem: Colao 2004's "about 4.5 %" has no published
numerator, and 4.5 % of 51 is 2.3 men. It is now stored as a narrative row that says the count is unknown,
rather than as a fabricated fraction.)*

- WADA. *Prohibited List 2026*, in force 1 Jan 2026. **S4.2** anti-estrogenic substances names Clomifene

  **S4.1** aromatase inhibitors names anastrozole; **S2.2.1** testosterone-stimulating peptides in males names

## 13. How these are legitimately obtained (US; researched 2026-10-20)

Information, not advice. Nothing below is a recommendation to take anything, and every decision and every
prescription here belongs to a licensed clinician who has seen your labs.

### 13.1 Regulatory status, item by item

| Drug | US status | What that means for a man who wants it |
|---|---|---|
| **Clomiphene citrate** | **FDA-approved** (1967, as Clomid/Serophene) — but **only for ovulation induction in women**. Never approved for any use in men. | Prescribing an approved drug for an unapproved indication is **off-label**, which is lawful for a licensed physician and routine here. The AUA guideline names it as an off-label option. Dispensed as an ordinary generic tablet from any pharmacy. |
| **Enclomiphene citrate** | **Not approved for anything.** The Androxal NDA drew a Complete Response Letter on **30 Nov 2015** and nothing has been approved since. | There is no FDA-approved enclomiphene product to prescribe, so a legitimate supply means a **compounding pharmacy** filling a prescription written for a named patient. See 13.2 — this is the item whose status people most often get wrong. |
| **hCG** | **Approved, prescription-only**, and since **23 March 2020** regulated as a **biologic** (BLA) rather than a drug, under the BPCIA "deemed to be a license" transition. | Brand-name/approved hCG on prescription is fine. **Compounded hCG is not**: biologics are outside the 503A and 503B compounding exemptions, so the compounded hCG that was widely sold (including for the "hCG diet") lost its legal route in 2020. FDA has told NABP that "federal law does not provide a legal pathway for marketing biological products… prepared outside the scope of an approved BLA." |
| **Anastrozole / letrozole** | Approved, prescription-only, **for breast cancer**. | Off-label in men, lawful to prescribe. Forge models zero muscle for these and a *fat cost*, and grades them the only `poor` tolerability in the group (spine bone loss). |
| **Tamoxifen** | Approved, prescription-only, **for breast cancer** (and risk reduction). | Off-label in men, lawful to prescribe. Carries a labelled VTE risk that has never been quantified in this use. |

**The enclomiphene compounding question, precisely, because the internet is confidently wrong about it in both
directions.** Three facts, all from FDA primary documents:

1. On **8 June 2022** FDA's Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee considered enclomiphene citrate for the
   503A Bulks List. FDA's own proposal was that it **not** be included. The committee voted **4 yes / 8 no /
   0 abstain** — i.e. against inclusion — with several members citing a **lack of clinical efficacy evidence**
   (not a safety concern). The committee is advisory; FDA is not bound by it.
2. FDA has **not acted on that vote**. On the 503A bulk-substances list **updated 14 May 2026**, enclomiphene
   citrate is still in **Category 1, "bulk drug substances under evaluation"** — the category where FDA states
   it does not intend to take enforcement action against a compounder who meets the guidance conditions.
3. Enclomiphene is **not** in Category 2 ("raise significant safety concerns"). That list currently holds
   cesium chloride, domperidone, germanium sesquioxide, **ibutamoren mesylate (MK-677)**, **kisspeptin-10** and
   quinacrine for intrauterine use. (Two of those, MK-677 and kisspeptin, *are* in this app's catalogue, and
   that is a genuine Category 2 restriction — just not on enclomiphene.)

There is also a second, independent argument for its compoundability that a PCAC member made on the record:
enclomiphene is a **component of an FDA-approved drug** (clomiphene citrate, which is 62 % enclomiphene), and
FD&C Act §503A(b)(1)(A) permits compounding from a bulk substance that is a component of an approved drug. The
Defense Department's Operation Supplement Safety states this position plainly (page current 12 Jan 2026): it
"can be used in compounding under Section 503A, but only with a valid prescription."

**Net:** compounded enclomiphene on a valid prescription is currently tolerated and widely dispensed, but it
rests on enforcement discretion plus a statutory argument, not on an approval. That status is revocable, and an
advisory committee has already voted against it once. No approved label means no approved dose, no
post-marketing safety surveillance, and quality that varies by pharmacy. Ask whether the pharmacy is
PCAB-accredited and whether it will provide a certificate of analysis.

### 13.2 The legitimate route, concretely

1. **See a clinician who does this work.** A **urologist** (ideally fellowship-trained in andrology or male
   infertility), an **endocrinologist**, or a reputable men's-health / telehealth practice. In Texas a
   telehealth prescription is lawful where the clinician is Texas-licensed and establishes a proper
   practitioner–patient relationship. The thing to screen a telehealth service on is whether it will tell you
   **no**: a service that requires real labs, repeats an abnormal testosterone, measures LH before choosing a
   drug, and is willing to conclude you do not need treatment is behaving like a clinic. One that ships a
   product after a questionnaire is behaving like a storefront.
2. **Labs first, and they are not optional.** Expect: **total testosterone, drawn fasted between 7 and 10 am**,
   plus **free testosterone, SHBG, LH, FSH, estradiol (sensitive assay), prolactin, CBC/hematocrit, PSA**
   (usually from 40), a **metabolic panel and lipids**, **TSH**, and **a semen analysis** if fertility is in
   play. This is the panel Forge's planner asks for.
3. **Why the repeat morning draw genuinely matters.** Testosterone is pulsatile and has a strong diurnal
   rhythm, so a single low reading means very little — guidelines require **two separate morning measurements**
   before diagnosing deficiency. It matters commercially too: a one-draw diagnosis is the standard way to
   convert a man with a normal level into a lifelong customer. And **LH is the decisive number**: low T with a
   normal or low LH is *secondary* and is what clomiphene, enclomiphene and hCG treat; low T with a clearly
   raised LH is *testicular*, and these drugs will not work.
4. **What follows.** A recheck at roughly 4–6 weeks and again at 3 months (total T, estradiol, hematocrit,
   LH/FSH), then periodically — plus PSA where age-appropriate, and semen analysis if fertility is the point.
   Stop and call the prescriber for **visual changes** on a SERM (blurring or flickering), and for leg swelling,
   chest pain or breathlessness on tamoxifen.
5. **The prescription is filled like any other.** Clomiphene, anastrozole, letrozole and tamoxifen are ordinary
   generics at any retail pharmacy. Enclomiphene comes from a **compounding pharmacy** the prescriber sends it
   to. hCG comes as an approved product.

**Insurance and cost.** Clomiphene for a man is off-label, so coverage is inconsistent, but it is a cheap old
generic: the median monthly copay in a 12,318-man US claims cohort was **$20** (range $0–$1,862; Guo 2019), and
cash discount-card prices are usually in the tens of dollars. **Enclomiphene is essentially never covered** —
it is not an approved drug — and compounded cash prices cluster around **$60–$120/month**, with telehealth
bundles that include the visit running higher; those figures come from commercial pharmacy and telehealth
sites rather than a published dataset, so treat them as a range to quote against, not a benchmark. Labs and the
consultation are extra, and a male-infertility workup is often covered where "low testosterone" alone is not.
Clomiphene costing roughly a tenth of enclomiphene, with far more evidence behind it, is a real part of this
decision — and it is exactly why the one tolerability study matters so much to people.

### 13.3 What to avoid, and why — factually

**"Research chemical" and "not for human consumption" sellers.** Enclomiphene is sold online with "Research
Facts" panels and "for research use only" labels, and sometimes as a dietary-supplement-style "testosterone
booster" with a Supplement Facts panel. Both are unlawful: **selling either clomiphene or enclomiphene as a
dietary-supplement ingredient is illegal**, and neither is an approved drug for this use, so a product marketed
for human consumption is an **unapproved new drug** (DoD Operation Supplement Safety, current 12 Jan 2026). The
"research use only" label is not a legal exemption; it is a marketing posture.

**The real risk is that you do not know what is in the bottle.** The best-documented analysis of this exact
market is **Van Wagoner 2017 in JAMA** (doi:10.1001/jama.2017.17069), which bought 44 products sold online as
SARMs and analysed them under WADA-accredited chain of custody:

- only **23 of 44 (52 %)** contained the substance advertised;
- **17 (39 %)** contained a *different* unapproved drug (including ibutamoren, GW501516 and SR9009);
- **4 (9 %)** contained **no active compound at all**;
- **11 (25 %)** contained substances **not listed on the label**;
- the labelled amount matched the measured amount in only **18 of 44 (41 %)** — i.e. **59 % were mis-dosed**.

That study tested SARMs, not SERMs — I could not find an equivalent published analysis of online enclomiphene
specifically, and I am not going to imply one exists. But it is the same sellers, the same "research chemical"
framing and the same absence of any testing requirement, and there is no reason to expect better. With an
unapproved drug there is no manufacturer to recall it, no adverse-event reporting, and no way to tell a
mis-dose from a non-response.

**Importing.** Under the FD&C Act, importing an unapproved drug is generally unlawful, including for personal
use and including foreign versions of US-approved drugs. FDA has a narrow **personal importation** enforcement
policy, but it is discretionary and its conditions (a serious condition with no effective domestic treatment,
no commercialisation to US consumers, a written personal-use statement, ≤3 months' supply) are not met by
wanting a cheaper testosterone drug — a domestic, approved, prescribable treatment exists. FDA also maintains
import alerts aimed at online pharmacies selling unapproved prescription drugs.

*I am deliberately not naming vendors, marketplaces, or ways around any of this.*

### 13.4 Anti-doping

If you ever race in a tested event — USADA/WADA-governed road racing included, and that is not hypothetical for
Bob — all of these are **prohibited at all times, in and out of competition**, under the WADA Prohibited List
2026 (in force 1 Jan 2026):

- **S4.2 Anti-estrogenic substances (anti-estrogens and SERMs)** — **clomifene** and **tamoxifen** are named;
  enclomiphene is clomifene's trans-isomer and the class is "including, but not limited to", so it is covered
  (and the DoD prohibited-ingredient list names enclomiphene directly). *Specified Substances.*
- **S4.1 Aromatase inhibitors** — anastrozole, letrozole. *Specified Substances.*
- **S2.2.1 Testosterone-stimulating peptides in males** — **chorionic gonadotrophin (CG)**, LH, GnRH agonists
  and kisspeptin. Prohibited **in males only**. Not a Specified Substance.
- **S1** — testosterone by any route.

A legitimate prescription does not make a prohibited substance permitted. It is the basis for a **Therapeutic
Use Exemption**, which has to be applied for, is granted on documented medical need, and is not granted simply
because a level is low-normal. Clomiphene is also a long-lived detection problem: zuclomiphene's ~30-day
half-life means it is detectable in urine for **months** after the last dose, so "I stopped before the season"
is not a plan. Enclomiphene's own half-life is ~10 h, but taking the isomer alone does not make it undetectable.

Ashwagandha, tongkat ali, cabergoline, sleeping more and eating enough are not prohibited.

### 13.5 The honest framing

For a 39-year-old man with **no labs**, the first legitimate step is not a drug at all — it is the morning
panel, repeated. Forge's planner says the same thing, and for Bob specifically it currently ranks every drug in
this section at a **score of zero**, because no trial has ever measured body composition on any SERM in a man
whose testosterone was already normal. The hormone moves; whether the muscle follows is untested. A clinician
with his numbers in front of them is the right person to decide what, if anything, to do about that.

## 14. The presentation fix, the audit of the discount chain, and the scenario band (2026-10-21)

Bob, on the planner: *"For the experimental drugs ... I'd really like it if the encyclopedia showed an
optimistic view, but it is what it is. Right now it's barely adding anything, and I just don't think that's
accurate. It helps a lot of people a lot."* Then: *"enclomiphene works for non hypogonadal males if there is
some specific issue that is causing low T, it's game changing for a lot of people. I want the game changing
results to be the default with the caveats mentioned."*

He was right twice, and only one of the two was a disagreement about evidence. The first was a bug.

### 14.1 The bug: the headline was the ranking score, not the estimate

For his profile (no labs, modeled baseline 548 ng/dL) the clomiphene row read:

> Clomiphene citrate · ℞ · Moderate · Extrapolated · Tolerability: moderate · **±0 lb net**
> › TOTAL T 548 → 823 … LEAN **+4.4 lb** ±0 to +5.4 over 12 mo · FAT −0.4 lb

The headline contradicted the body of its own row. `±0` was `row.score`, which for an item whose
body-composition effect has never been measured is deliberately pinned to the **conservative end** of the row's
own range (zero) so that a drug with a published hormone rise and no body-composition trial cannot outrank
something that was actually weighed. That is a defensible **ranking** rule. It should never have been the
**displayed** number.

Fixed: every row now carries `net = { lb, lo, hi }` — the modeled central estimate of lean minus fat, with its
range — and that is what the row leads with. `score` still exists and still ranks. They are different numbers
and the code says so. Bob's row now reads **+4.3 lb net · 0 to +5.4**, with the Extrapolated badge, the
tolerability grade, the zero-floored range, the "start assumed" marker and every note unchanged.

A second face of the same bug: the UI **bucketed** rows on the sign of `score`. Because `score` is
`leanLo − fatHi` and the 12-month projection puts about 0.1 lb of fat on the high side, clomiphene scored −0.1
and was filed under *"Changes something, but not in your favour"* — a drug the same page credits with +4.4 lb
of lean. Buckets are now chosen by **evidence class** (was body composition measured in a man like this one?)
and the **sign of the central estimate**, which is what those headings actually claim.

### 14.2 Audit of the testosterone → lean chain, for a sustained 548 → 823 ng/dL

Every discount, measured rather than reasoned about:

| # | Discount | Bites for Bob? | Cost | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `tPctByBaseline` taper above 550 ng/dL | No (he sits at the anchor) | 0 | Justified; labelled an assumption where it does bite |
| 2 | `tCI [0.70, 1.30]` (Hohl 2025's CI) | Range only | 0 on the point estimate | Justified |
| 3 | Credit bound `bcBoundOf` = max(`bcMaxLevel` 500, `bcExtrapMaxLevel` 1390) | **No** — 823 < 1390 | 0 | n/a for him |
| 4 | `freeAdj`, capped at 1 | **No** — computes to exactly 1.000 | 0 | Not double-counting; see 14.3 |
| 5 | `tAgeResponse(39)` = 0.98 | Yes | −2% | Justified (Bhasin 2005: older men nearly as responsive) |
| 6 | Saturating time course, τ = 12 wk | Yes, correctly | — | Justified; reproduces the 20-week fit at 20 weeks |
| 7 | **Serum ramp advancing the accrual clock** | **Yes** | **−20% of the asymptote, permanently** | **Double-counting. Removed.** |
| 8 | Range floor to zero when `extrapolated` | Yes, on the range | 0 on the point estimate | Keep — this is the honesty |
| 9 | `score = leanLo − fatHi` as the headline | Yes | −100% of the headline | **The bug. Fixed (14.1).** |

So: **the central estimate was not being double-discounted by the credit logic.** The Bhasin dose-response is
applied in full — ln(823/548) × 3.53 kg/ln × 0.98 = 1.409 kg = **3.10 lb at 20 weeks**, which is exactly what
`testosteroneEffect` returns. Bob's intuition that the model was suppressing the number was right about the
*displayed* number and wrong about the *modeled* one, with one real exception, #7.

### 14.3 `freeAdj` is inert, not a discount

`freeAdj = clamp((freeT/freeTBase) / (level/T0), 0, 1)` was added to answer the objection that a SERM raises
SHBG, so free testosterone might move less than total. Measured across the catalogue, uncapped:

| item | T0 | level | SHBG | uncapped `freeAdj` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| clomiphene | 548 | 823 | 35 → 39 | **1.015** |
| clomiphene | 300 | 573 | 35 → 39 | 1.021 |
| enclomiphene | 548 | 763 | 35 → 38 | 1.014 |
| hCG | 548 | 1,100 | 35 (no change) | 1.165 |
| tamoxifen | 548 | 790 | 35 | 1.074 |

It is **≥ 1 for every item at every baseline tested**, so the cap at 1 means it can only ever be a no-op, and it
is. It costs exactly zero. That is the correct answer and it matches the measurement the catalogue already
cites — Bremner 1987 found the bioavailable fraction went 65% → 66% of total on clomiphene, i.e. unchanged. The
cap stays, because crediting the >1 values would run the Bhasin curve (which is denominated in **total** T)
on a quantity it was not fitted to. Nothing to remove.

### 14.4 The one real double-discount: the serum ramp was docking the asymptote

The accrual curve is saturating, `cumulative(w) = A·(1 − e^(−w/τ))`, so the rate decays as `e^(−w/τ)` and almost
all of the gain is earned early. The endogenous items also ramp their serum level in over `rampWeeks` (2–6
weeks to plateau — measured, and right). Evaluating a decaying rate at **raw elapsed weeks** while the amplitude
`A` is still ramping means the highest-rate weeks are spent at a reduced amplitude, and the clock has already
run past them by the time the level arrives. The amplitude recovers; the decay does not.

Measured, for Bob on clomiphene (548 → 823 ng/dL), `stackTimeline().cumLeanDirect`:

| weeks | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| 12 | 2.00 lb | 2.00 lb |
| 20 | 2.31 | 2.92 |
| 52 | 3.00 | **3.81** |
| 104 | 3.05 | 3.88 |
| 416 | **3.05 — for ever** | 3.88 |

Against an undamped Bhasin asymptote of **3.83 lb**. A six-week titration was permanently removing 0.77 lb,
about 20%, and no trial supports that: nothing in Bhasin 1996/2001 says a slower titration costs you muscle you
would otherwise have built. A ramp should **delay** a trajectory, not dock its end point. The
exogenous-testosterone branch in the same file never had the bug, because it feeds the full level from week 0
and lets τ do the timing — so this was also an inconsistency between two uses of one curve.

Fix (`rampExposureLoss` in `js/engine/metabolic.js`): the accrual clock counts **effective exposure** — elapsed
time minus the exposure lost while the level was still climbing, which is `∫(1 − ramp)`, i.e. `w − w²/(2R)`
while `w ≤ R` and a constant `R/2` afterwards. A 6-week ramp now delays the curve by 3 weeks and converges on
the same asymptote. The titration still costs time (20 weeks reaches 76% of the asymptote, not 100%); it no
longer costs muscle.

### 14.5 Duration: the extrapolation to 12 months is sound

The Bhasin curves are 20 weeks and the row projects 12 months. The model reaches the 20-week fit value **at 20
weeks** by construction (τ = 12 wk gives `sat = 1 − e^(−20/12) = 0.811`, and `A = lean20/sat`), then adds only
the remaining ~23% over the following 32 weeks. Nothing is silently truncated: the projection path steps week
by week through `modifiersAt` and accumulates, and `stackTimeline` sums the weekly rates. The week-52 modifiers
object shows a near-zero instantaneous rate because the asymptote has been reached, which is correct and is not
what the cumulative figure is computed from.

### 14.6 Bounds that bite for OTHER items, and why they stay

| item | modeled level at T0 = 548 | credit clipped to | why that is not an extra body-composition penalty |
|---|---|---|---|
| clomiphene | 823 | 823 (no clip) | `bcExtrapMaxLevel` 1,390 = Tenover 1991's measured maximum |
| enclomiphene | 763 | **604** | 604 is the highest **morning** group mean ever reported (Wiehle 2013 day-42 C0); 764 is a 24-h Cmax, and Forge's serum numbers are morning levels. The clip corrects a known generosity in the *level* model, it is not a second penalty on the muscle |
| hCG | 1,100 | **870** | `ceilingNg` 1,100 is explicitly an **assumption** (top of the male reference range); 870 is the highest level actually observed (Liu 2002, inferred). Same reasoning |

Both were left alone. Removing them would be optimism, not accuracy. They are now visible in the scenario band,
which is why enclomiphene reads as weak at a mid-normal baseline and strong at a suppressed one — that is the
evidence, not a thumb on the scale.

### 14.7 The scenario band: stop leading with one invented baseline

Bob's second point. **Functional secondary hypogonadism** is a real clinical entity, not a loophole:
Grossmann & Matsumoto 2017 (J Clin Endocrinol Metab 102:1067–75, doi:10.1210/jc.2016-3580) — *"such men do not
demonstrate anatomical hypothalamic-pituitary-testicular axis pathology but have functional hypogonadism that
is potentially reversible."* And it is exactly the population the only SERM body-composition result was
measured in (Soares 2018, obese men, T 226 ± 70).

The reference range makes the point by itself. **Travison 2017** (J Clin Endocrinol Metab 102:1161–73,
doi:10.1210/jc.2016-2935), 9,054 men across four US/European cohorts cross-calibrated to the CDC reference
method: in healthy **non-obese** men 19–39 the 2.5th / 5th / 50th / 95th / 97.5th percentiles are
**264 / 303 / 531 / 852 / 916 ng/dL**. End to end that is a **3.5-fold** spread. A man at 350 is "normal" on
every lab report ever printed, sits near the 10th percentile, and can be a long way below his own set point.

So the planner now opens with a **band of four scenarios** rather than one point estimate
(`scenarioBand()` in `js/engine/tplan.js`): suppressed ≈300, low-normal ≈400, population average ≈550,
high-normal ≈750, each with a normal LH (which is what makes it the secondary/functional case these drugs
treat). Each column re-runs the **same row builder** against a synthetic profile, so its lean and fat come
through `project()` exactly like the man's own rows. For a man Bob's size, 12 months:

| baseline | enclomiphene | clomiphene | clomiphene + hCG |
|---|---|---|---|
| **≈300 suppressed** | +8.4 lb net (0 to +10.4) · extrapolated | **+10.2 lb net (+7.8 to +12.3) · MEASURED** | **+15.8 lb net (+11.1 to +21) · MEASURED** |
| ≈400 low-normal | +6.2 (0 to +7.9) · extrapolated | +8.0 (0 to +9.2) · extrapolated | +12.9 · measured |
| ≈550 average | +1.4 (−0.1 to +1.4) · extrapolated | +6.0 (0 to +7.1) · extrapolated | +7.7 (0 to +9.3) · extrapolated |
| ≈750 high-normal | ±0 · extrapolated | +4.4 (0 to +5.2) · extrapolated | +4.9 · extrapolated |

**The line that is not crossed.** The assumed default baseline is unchanged (`averageTotalT`, 548 for a
39-year-old). Every column is labelled a scenario; exactly one is marked "closest to what Forge assumes for
you" and with no lab that is the ≈550 column; his own rows below still run entirely off his own level. A man
whose LH says **primary** gets no column marked as his at all and a note saying why, because every column is a
secondary case and his own rows are correctly modeled at zero.

### 14.8 Reversible causes, and this man's own risk factors

A SERM and fixing the cause attack the same problem from opposite ends — the cause fix raises the set-point,
the SERM overrides the feedback the cause acts through — so the planner names the causes with the same weight
as the drugs, says whether Forge can **see** each one in this man's data (`yes` / `maybe` / `no` / `unknown`),
and links to the catalogue item that models it: obesity → `fat_loss`, sleep debt → `sleep_hygiene`, low energy
availability → `energy_availability`, endurance volume → `endurance_load`, alcohol → `alcohol`, raised
prolactin → `cabergoline`, high SHBG masking a low free T (no item; the fix is to measure free T), and
**opioids**, which Forge does **not** model and says so — prevalence of androgen deficiency on chronic opioid
therapy is 20–80% and the mechanism is inhibition of GnRH synthesis and secretion (Kafel 2025, Andrology
13:2010–21, doi:10.1111/andr.70013).

Separately, `riskFlags()` names what in *this* profile makes a below-average baseline plausible — a big
endurance background (`cardioTargetMin` ≥ 150), an appetite-suppressing stimulant in the stack, and the low
energy availability that follows from both and that Forge currently models at **zero** because no deficit has
been entered. The verdict line says in terms that these make a low baseline **plausible**, *"They do not make
it a fact"*, and that a lab settles it. None of this moves a number.

### 14.9 "Testicular" was being misread

Bob read the lab-panel line *"low T with a high LH is testicular"* and replied: *"I don't have small testicles
as far as I can tell."* That is a fair reading of a bad word, and it could lead someone to dismiss a real
finding. Everywhere the app used "primary"/"testicular" as a bare term it now says that LH is the pituitary's
**instruction**, that a high LH with a low T means the instruction is being sent loudly and not obeyed, and
that this is **not visible from outside** — most of a testis is seminiferous tubules and the Leydig cells that
make testosterone are a small fraction of its volume, so they can underperform at a normal size (age-related
Leydig decline, past varicocele, mumps orchitis, trauma, chemotherapy). The **compensated** state (raised LH,
still-normal testosterone; Tajar 2010, doi:10.1210/jc.2009-1796) is named as the real intermediate it is, and
as the one a man in his late thirties is most likely to drift into.

### 14.10 The catalogue as an encyclopedia

`detailSheet` rendered mechanism, effects, risks, tolerability, typical use and sources, and stopped. Every
other field in the catalogue was dead data. The sheet now also renders, for every item that has them: the
**body-composition evidence class** with its bounds and `bcNote`; what the item does to **estradiol and SHBG**;
who it **suits and does not**; **interactions** (new field, each tagged pharmacodynamic / pharmacokinetic /
additive); **what is not known** (new field); and time course, fertility, monitoring and anti-doping. Browse
rows now carry the evidence grade, the body-composition class and the tolerability grade, so an entry can be
triaged without opening it.

The one genuinely important **pharmacokinetic** interaction in the category is now documented: tamoxifen is a
prodrug requiring CYP2D6 to form endoxifen, and strong CYP2D6 inhibitors — paroxetine and fluoxetine among them
— severely impair that conversion (Brauch 2009, Clin Chem 55:1770–82, doi:10.1373/clinchem.2008.121756).
Cabergoline, which had a single citation, gained the Endocrine Society guideline (Melmed 2011,
doi:10.1210/jc.2010-1692) and the randomized trial that made it first-line (Webster 1994, N Engl J Med
331:904–9, doi:10.1056/NEJM199410063311403).

### 14.11 Where Bob's intuition is not supported

Stated plainly, because the point is accuracy:

1. **The model was not systematically suppressing the clomiphene number.** Apart from the ramp artefact in
   14.4, the Bhasin curve was being applied in full. The problem was the headline, not the model.
2. **A mid-normal baseline really does mean a smaller modeled effect**, and that is evidence, not pessimism.
   The measured SERM effect is a roughly constant number of ng/dL (Hohl 2025; neither Keihani 2020 nor Kim 2026
   found baseline total T predicted the response), so the *percentage* shrinks as the baseline rises — and the
   lean credit runs off a **log** ratio, so it shrinks again.
3. **"It helps a lot of people a lot" is true for hypogonadal men and unproven for eugonadal ones.** Nobody has
